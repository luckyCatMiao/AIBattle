## Implemented changes

entry.json selects {"mode":"program","command":["python3","engine.py"]}. The engine handles successive JSON lines and flushes one JSON move per request. Input boards remain authoritative. previous_bot.py is untouched. The prior submitted engine and review are preserved as engine_iteration1_astra.py and review_iteration1_astra.md. No network, opponent files, downloaded engines, or subagents were used.

1. Expand root candidates beyond the 16 quiet-move beam to retain every own or opponent move scoring at least 12,000, including developing open threes. Previously only scores at least 90,000 survived that cutoff. The old threshold already retained double threes; lowering it merely to 70,000 would not address the intended gap. The deeper search retains its original forcing-candidate threshold, avoiding an uncontrolled branching increase. This broadens attacking development and preventive defense at the most important decision point; it is still heuristic, not an exhaustive three-threat solver.
2. Maintain two incremental color-character views of the board. Line evaluation joins those views rather than repeatedly decoding board cells for both players. Make/undo update and restore both views. A local 16-move make/undo comparison on latest-game positions improved from approximately 3.0 to 2.3 ms and 2.5 to 1.9 ms. These small measurements suggest reduced evaluation cost, not a guaranteed search or playing-strength gain.
3. Raise iterative search's maximum depth from seven to twelve while preserving the existing 4.4-second deadline and last completed result. Cheap constrained positions can continue deeper instead of stopping arbitrarily at seven. Hard positions still stop on the deadline. An earlier regression reached depth eight, confirming the old ceiling is no longer enforced.

## Remaining weaknesses and future notes

Quiet move selection, radius-two neighborhoods, static evaluation, and depth-boundary extensions remain selective. Retained open-three moves increase root width and may reduce completed depth. There is no exhaustive all-defense solver for attacks combining threes and forcing fours. A timed-out proof means unknown, not safe. Opening preferences remain unproved, and two wins against Astra are too little evidence to tune openings confidently. Runtime measurements apply to this host, with approximately 0.6 seconds of reserve under the five-second referee limit.

For the next round, prioritize the first avoidable loss of initiative in the new pair. In particular, distinguish a forcing counterattack that buys tempo from one that actually neutralizes the surviving fork threats. Keep exact forced-defense and multi-four regressions, deduplicate full records and shared prefixes, and compare candidate-width changes against completed search depth before widening deeper search.
